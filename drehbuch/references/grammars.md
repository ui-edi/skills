# Grammars, signature move, aesthetic range

The device kit decides how an act moves. The **grammar** decides how the whole page is structured: navigation, order, hero and ending. Choose it on purpose, or every page ends up with the same skeleton.

## Page grammars

| Grammar | Feels like | Navigation | Hero | Ending | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Filmic** | a trailer | minimal or none | big layered scene | one action on a held final frame | more than 7 acts, two scrubs back to back |
| **Chapters** | a magazine story | chapter list or sticky chapter title | strong headline, image second | a short colophon plus action | pinning every chapter |
| **Poster** | a typographic poster | none | one huge statement | the statement resolves | images competing with type |
| **Gallery** | a shop window / catalogue | filter or index | the best item, big | "see all" or the action | long pinned acts |
| **Split stage** | a demo next to its explanation | tabs or steps | product on one side, text on the other | the product in its final state | full-screen video |
| **One world** | a single continuous place | none | entering the world | arriving somewhere | random cuts; only for briefs that ask for "one journey" |

Pick one, say in one line why the brief points there. Mixing is fine if one grammar clearly leads.

## The signature move

One interaction that exists only on this page and could not be moved to another brand without losing its meaning. It comes from the brief's "It's the site where ___" sentence.

Counts:
- Scrolling digs a tunnel through the earth on an ant game's page.
- The round timer on a boxing-timer page counts down as you scroll through one round, and the bell rings visually at the end.
- A tea page where the leaves unfold as the scroll pours the water.

Does not count:
- A default effect with a new colour.
- A cursor that leaves a trail.
- A parallax hero (that is the baseline, not a signature).

Write it in page code with `Drehbuch.on()`. Keep it lightweight and make sure the page still works (and still makes sense) without it under reduced motion.

## Don't repeat yourself

If the user has had pages built with this skill before, compare the new plan with the old ones on six points: grammar, navigation, hero device, act order, ending, signature move. Change at least four. Two pages from the same person that share the same skeleton look like a template.

## Aesthetic range

Premium-minimal is one option, not the default. Offer the range and let the brief decide:

- **Premium-minimal:** lots of space, one accent, quiet type.
- **Editorial:** magazine grid, serif headlines, pull quotes.
- **Playful:** rounded shapes, bright colours, bouncy motion (still readable).
- **Retro / pixel:** pixel fonts, limited palette, chunky borders; great for pixel-art games.
- **Brutalist:** raw grids, big type, hard edges.
- **Maximalist:** dense, layered, colourful, but with a clear reading path.
- **Natural / organic:** earthy colours, soft textures, slow motion.

The product's own look comes first: a pixel-art game gets a page that feels like the game, not a SaaS landing page.
